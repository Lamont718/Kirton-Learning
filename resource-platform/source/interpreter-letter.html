Ask for an interpreter. In writing.

Fill in four boxes and print a letter in your language and English, requesting an interpreter for your child's IEP meeting and a translated copy of the IEP. Hand it to the school. Keep a copy.

This is a right, not a favor. Parents in New York City have the right, on request, to interpretation at special education meetings and translation of IEPs, evaluations, and notices. You should never be asked to bring your own interpreter, and your child should never be used as your interpreter.

How to use it

Four steps, and keep proof of every one.

1

Print two copies

One for the school, one for you. If you can't print, copy the text and send it as an email — email is written proof too.

2

Hand it in at least 72 hours before the meeting

Give it to the school secretary, your child's IEP team, or the CSE office. Ask them to date-stamp your copy.

3

Write down the date you gave it in

On your own copy. If the interpreter doesn't show up, that date is the most useful thing you own.

4

If nothing happens, call

Use the numbers below. If it still doesn't get fixed, call Advocates for Children — free, and this is exactly what they do.
