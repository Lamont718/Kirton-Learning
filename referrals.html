The short version

  • I work the academic goals in an IEP — reading, writing, math. That's the whole of what I do.
  • Speech, OT, PT, counseling, behavior work and evaluations are licensed professions. I hold none of those licenses, so I don't do any of it.
  • This page is where I put the names instead. It costs you nothing and it costs them nothing.
  • Nobody pays to be on this list and I receive nothing if you call them. No fees, no referral arrangements, no sponsored placement. That is not a courtesy — it's the law in New York, and it's the only reason a list like this is worth reading.
  • I list a license number and the date I checked it, so you can check it too.

Most IEPs contain two kinds of goals: academic ones, and related-service ones. If your daughter has a speech goal and reading and math goals, the speech goal belongs to a licensed speech-language pathologist — and the reading and math are exactly what I do. She isn't a bad fit for me. She just has more than one person on her team, and this page is for finding the others.

Something you may already be owed

A lot of families pay out of pocket for a service their child's IEP already mandates. It's worth knowing how that's supposed to work.

When a district can't staff a related service that's written into an IEP — speech, OT, PT, counseling — it can issue a Related Services Authorization, an RSA. An RSA lets you find an independent provider yourself and have the district pay for it. P-3 and P-4 vouchers work similarly for other mandated services.

That's the mechanism, stated plainly. It is not advice about your child's situation. I can't tell you whether your district has done anything wrong, whether you qualify, or how a case would come out — that's advocacy and legal work, and I don't do either. Ask your child's IEP team or the Committee on Special Education, start at schools.nyc.gov, and know that there are advocacy organizations in this city that help families with exactly this for free.
